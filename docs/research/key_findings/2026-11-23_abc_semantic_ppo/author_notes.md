# ABC router candidate features

1. **Continue productive contact drive** measures how much the candidate retains a recent observed push or rotation role during visible gap closure. It uses sampled contact and drive stage, with a weaker fallback for sustained contact when the stage is missing. Changing the peer reduces the score, since a joint commitment may affect drive.
2. **Continue active contact preparation** measures whether the candidate retains a role showing recent robot travel or activity in a public setup stage. Free MOVE gets this signal when the robot has also been closing its target-relative distance. The feature can be positive before object progress starts. It discounts older observations and uses a weaker fallback for missing stages.
3. **Change repeated stalled intent** is positive for departing from an affected role's recent direction or controller after accumulated visible evidence of weak gap closure with contact drive or a stationary stage. It is negative when a candidate repeats that intent. Evidence of preparation or useful driving discounts the stall signal. Speed-only changes count as continuation. Dropping all active commands does not earn change credit.
4. **Paired push opposition** measures the opposing component of simultaneous translational intents, weighted by their nominal speed balance. Its range is −1 to 0. It is a geometric command cue. Contacts and the controller may deliver different forces.

The 24 supplied traces contain 12 examples per recorded outcome. Across the initial excerpts, median gap closure is 0.185 m for the non-recovered group and 0.256 m for the recovered group. The traces include multiple standoff and final boundaries before sustained pushing, as well as runs that stay at an identical gap through repeated `find` or `wait_peer_contact`. Both outcome groups contain these behaviors. The reduced examples show `move` commands in the 48–79 block with four slots per compass direction. Only the observed direction convention is used for continuity. The offline examples motivated the feature design. Their outcome, room fields and richer execution records are absent at runtime.

All features use up to four completed contiguous decision intervals. Object-specific command comparisons require association with the queried target. The visible gap can be noisy, and a stage sample gives only the boundary state. Target-relative distance is a coarse preparation cue that can miss useful lateral detours. Productive rotations can leave the gap flat, especially over a short history. The numeric scales are design choices based on metres and control ticks. Candidate duration is excluded because it does not establish a new intent and its execution cost depends on hidden controller state. This pass used stored observations and no simulation.
