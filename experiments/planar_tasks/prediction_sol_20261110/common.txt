Additional preregistered diagnostic brief, shared by both experimental conditions.

This is one centralized shared-sensor program commanding two robot options. The
three development families are open-space transport, release from near a fixed
wall followed by transport, and transport around a fixed obstacle. Obstacles are
not movable. Family names identify development fixtures only; deployment receives
no family, task identifier, map, or object ground truth. Infer relevant situations
from legal observations and history. Do not hardcode fixture coordinates.

PUSH already includes local surface selection, approach, coordinated contact and
push execution. It is not a bare force command. Do not require a separate contact
acquisition action before every PUSH. Legal action availability is not a promise
of task progress. The same command continues controller state; option completion,
failure and pauses must be interpreted through available execution feedback.
You may use any legal option and autonomous experiment. No rotation, route,
recovery sequence, contact assignment, or geometric threshold is supplied here.

Account for three task outcomes: transporting the object to a requested region
and settling there; escaping a wall-constrained configuration sufficiently to
continue transport; and passing an obstacle with a feasible continuation towards
the goal. A command being issued, a robot moving, or a local controller completing
is not by itself evidence that these task outcomes occurred. Estimate effects
only from the legal sensors/history. Apparent failure can involve geometry
estimation, robot accessibility, low-level execution, or action-effect prediction.

Development coverage protocol (identical for both conditions): allocate resources
for a final regression of ONE selected candidate on all nine named development
fixtures, each using test_policy with max_control_ticks=1800 and max_decisions=1800.
This costs at most 9 tests, 9 resets and 16200 reserved control ticks within the
same 20-test/40-reset/36000-tick total. Plan exploration accordingly. You may use
the remaining budget to revise and retest; report any fixtures not tested with
the exact submitted package. Never describe tests of old revisions as coverage
of the submitted program. The final candidate remains your choice, even if it
fails. Do not run out the budget without submitting.

Submit a short DIAGNOSTIC.json alongside your program, recording your chosen
architecture, observed development outcomes and unresolved hypotheses. It is an
author report, not an evaluator. Do not include speculative results as measured.
If useful, emit compact JSON diagnostic events to stderr on meaningful strategy
changes (not every physics tick), including decision, mode, reason and estimated
subgoal. This is optional, has no effect on task scoring, and receives no privileged
feedback. Do not change the exact action response schema.
